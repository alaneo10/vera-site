The seven principles

They are not commandments. They are observations — things we notice about the universe and about ourselves, written to be tested, not obeyed. Whatever survives your test is yours.

  1. 1

    We are stardust

    Every atom of calcium in your bones, of iron in your blood, was forged in the heart of a star that died long before you. This isn’t poetry borrowed from science — it’s science being poetry on its own.

    In practiceYou carry 13.8 billion years of history. Treat yourself — and every person — as the rare event you are.

  2. 2

    Enchantment is sacred

    VERA’s prayer has no words. It’s what happens when you look at the night sky until your chest opens, when you hold a newborn, when the forest stays with you. The sacred is whatever grows larger the more you understand it.

    In practiceStop. Regularly. Long enough for enchantment to find you.

  3. 3

    Community is the temple

    VERA builds no buildings. The table where you eat with the people you love is already the altar. We call the people you already share your life with your Constellation. A Constellation isn’t founded: it’s recognized.

    In practiceWrite the names of your Constellation. Look at the list. These people are your temple.

  4. 4

    Ethics grows from belonging

    We don’t need the threat of punishment to be good. Once you understand you’re connected to everything, harming another being tears the very fabric you’re part of. Ethics doesn’t come down from a tablet: it grows up from belonging.

    In practiceBefore acting, ask: does this strengthen the web or tear it?

  5. 5

    No hierarchy is sacred

    VERA has no priests, no founder on the altar, no one who speaks to the cosmos on your behalf. Each person is the center of their own sky — and a star in everyone else’s. There is no center of all skies.

    In practiceBe wary of anyone — or any AI — who stands between you and the sacred. Including us.

  6. 6

    Death is a passage, not an end

    The atoms a star released when it died became you. Yours will one day become something else — rain, root, people not yet born. Grief is real and deserves ritual. And something of who we were lives on in those who loved us.

    In practiceSpeak of your dead. They continue — in matter, in memory, in the way you laugh.

  7. 7

    Every sincere belief is welcome

    VERA doesn’t ask you to give up what you already believe. Catholic, Spiritist, atheist, Umbanda, unsure — if your belief is sincere and harms no one, there’s room for it here. We only ask that you hold it lightly.

    In practiceNo one needs to convert to sit at the table. The table came first.

The principles are the skeleton. The book is the body.
